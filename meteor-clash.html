METEORCLASH

Genre: tap defense / tower defense hybrid. Platform: iPhone (Unity 6). Status: playable build, personal and unreleased.

Meteors fall toward your planet. You tap them out of the sky, spend the Metal they drop on turrets and economy buildings, and carry Stardust into permanent upgrades between runs. Five planets, each harder and better paying than the last.

I designed the systems, economy and balance, and built it with AI coding tools. I modeled every 3D asset myself in Blender.

Gameplay

Mechanics

Tap meteors before they hit your planet. Spend what they drop, survive longer, and come back stronger.

Three currencies

Metal
Dropped by every meteor. Spent during the run on upgrades, turrets and buildings. Resets each run.
Stardust
Dropped by every meteor and paid by challenges. Kept between runs for permanent upgrades.
Shimmer
Only from gold meteors and weekly challenges. Scarce on purpose: late upgrades, boosters, a revive.

What makes a run interesting

Tap streak.

Hit meteors without letting the timer run out. At 5, 10, 20, 40 and 70 hits the streak raises tap damage and income, from 1.1x up to 1.5x. A planet hit ends it.

Crits and multi-hit.

Upgrades give each tap a chance to crit for up to 5x, or to hit up to four more meteors at once.

Gold meteors and Gold Fever.

Destroying a gold meteor starts Gold Fever: Metal and score are multiplied by 2 to 4 for 8 to 20 seconds, but meteors spawn twice as fast and fly 25% faster.

Bonus waves.

Wave 10 brings the first gold meteor. From wave 15, every fifth wave is a bonus wave with two to five of them.

Every wave is harder.

More meteors each wave (13% more on Planet 1, 33% on Planet 5), each tougher and faster. Bigger meteor types join in, and they pay more.

Five planets.

Each planet multiplies meteor health (up to 7x), planet damage (up to 8x) and rewards (up to 4x), and brings the big meteors earlier.

Build slots.

Windmills open three building slots each. Turrets and cities fill them. Cities pay a share of each wave's income; the Power Plant boosts cities and turrets.

Cap and Starter upgrades.

Permanent upgrades come in pairs. A Cap raises how far an upgrade can go inside a run. A Starter begins the run with levels already bought.

Planet defence.

Hull, repair, a damage shield and a magnetic field that slows meteors. One upgrade simply ignores the first hits of each wave.

Boosters and challenges.

Three daily and two weekly challenges pay Stardust and Shimmer. Boosters cost 15 Shimmer and last an hour: double Stardust, triple tap damage, or half planet damage.

The assets

Every model is mine, built low-poly on purpose. Triangle counts are under each one.

Drag or use the arrow keys to rotate. Pinch or use the plus and minus buttons to zoom.

Turrets

Base values, before upgrades.

Each turret you place raises the price of the next one, whatever its type. Fifteen turrets at most.

Buildings

Six balance changes I made

The numbers above did not start there. These are the changes that moved the game the most.

  1. A missed tap no longer breaks the streak.

    What I saw
    One stray tap wiped out a long streak. Fast play was being punished.
    What I changed
    A streak now ends only when its timer runs out, a meteor hits the planet, or the wave ends.
    Why
    The streak should reward staying on target, not punish a slip of the thumb.

  2. Streak bonus cut in half.

    What I saw
    The streak multiplied damage and income together, so a good streak snowballed and the rest of the run stopped mattering.
    What I changed
    Top bonus from 3.0x at 60 hits to 1.5x at 70, across five tiers instead of six.
    Why
    A bonus should feel good without replacing the upgrades.

  3. Bonus waves twice as often, with a cap.

    What I saw
    Gold meteors are the exciting moment, and they came around too rarely.
    What I changed
    Bonus waves moved from every tenth wave to every fifth, with at most five gold meteors.
    Why
    More frequent highs, while the cap keeps Shimmer scarce.

  4. Every turret makes the next one cost more.

    What I saw
    With flat prices the best move was to fill the planet with turrets and stop tapping.
    What I changed
    Prices now climb with every turret placed: a Flak goes from 150 to 5,000 Metal by the fifteenth.
    Why
    Tapping stays the core of the game, and each turret is a real decision.

  5. Later planets got much harder.

    What I saw
    A player strong enough to finish one planet walked through the next.
    What I changed
    After three revisions, meteor health now runs from 1x on Planet 1 to 7x on Planet 5, and planet damage from 1x to 8x.
    Why
    A new planet should send you back to the permanent upgrades.

  6. Stardust first, Shimmer later.

    What I saw
    Asking for the rare currency early stalled new players.
    What I changed
    Permanent upgrades cost only Stardust for their first levels. Shimmer enters at level 10 and ramps from there.
    Why
    Early progress runs on the common currency, and the rare one becomes a long-term goal.
